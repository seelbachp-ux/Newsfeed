**Nvidia reinstates Jensen Huang as CEO, and the stock jumps 31%** — On Monday, Nvidia's board brought Huang back as CEO and president, two weeks after he resigned. The board said it had taken input from "senior levels of the United States government" and that his return poses no national security risk. President Trump said he spoke to the board "directly and indirectly" and gave it assurances on national security. NVDA closed at $165.05, up 31.5%, its best day since 2003, adding nearly $1T in market value. The Nasdaq rose about 6.5%, which pulled it back from the edge of a bear market. The reported SEC accounting probe is still open.
*Why it matters:* Friday's selloff in AI hardware reversed in a single session. The way it happened, with the White House openly pushing a chipmaker's board, adds a new political risk to the AI supply chain.
Source: [Washington Post](https://www.washingtonpost.com/technology/2026/11/23/nvidia-board-reinstates-jensen-huang-after-us-government-input/)

**Anthropic launches Claude Ship: agents that review, fix and approve pull requests** — Claude Ship has three parts. Code Review is generally available. PR Steward, in beta, fixes failing CI, merge conflicts and review feedback, and comments with how to roll back each push. Claude Approvals, also in beta, signs off on routine changes under a policy written in plain language in a file in the repo. It works with GitHub repos only and is rolling out to Team and Enterprise plans. It offers review depths of Quick, Standard and Deep, monthly spending caps, and an observe-only mode to start with. No pricing has been published.
*Why it matters:* agents that merge code, not just suggest it, are now a product from a major vendor. The policy-in-repo model is worth borrowing even on a solo project.
Source: [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/23/anthropic-launches-claude-ship-letting-ai-agents-review-fix-and-approve-code-changes/)

**OpenAI is testing "Dots," agents that keep working between chats** — Dots are personal agents that carry on with tasks after the conversation ends. They are in limited testing on the $20 ChatGPT Plus tier and are expanding to Business Standard.
*Why it matters:* consumer assistants are moving from answering questions to running background tasks. That raises the bar for what users will expect an in-app AI feature to do.
Source: [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2322656/openai-is-testing-dots-personal-agents-on-its-20-per-month-plus-tier/)

**Google launches Agent Compute Engine, a managed sandbox service for agents** — Each agent runs in its own small VM. Credentials are kept outside the agent's workspace, and suspended sessions resume from checkpoints.
*Why it matters:* keeping secrets out of the agent's reach directly addresses the credential leaks that hit AI providers last week.
Source: [InfoQ](https://www.infoq.com/news/2026/11/google-agent-compute-engine/)

**Startup says AI agents designed a whole new CPU architecture, "Frostbite"** — Architect Labs says its agents designed a general-purpose processor with a new instruction set. In pre-silicon simulation it claims 4.59× the performance per watt of an in-order core, or 3.83× on 15 untuned workloads. No chip has been built, and the core it was compared against is not named.
*Why it matters:* if the claim holds up, agents are moving from writing software to designing the hardware it runs on. For now it is a simulation result to treat with caution.
Source: [Tom's Hardware](https://www.tomshardware.com/tech-industry/semiconductors/ai-agents-design-an-entirely-new-cpu-architecture-from-scratch-startup-claims-frostbite-posts-4-59x-the-performance-per-watt-of-an-in-order-core-in-pre-silicon-tests)
